Celebrini Is Winning It All: Faceoffs, Games, and (As We Now Know) Will Smith's Heart

By Poppy Pond

It may only be November, but the NHL season is already firing on all cylinders. And few players embody that better than Macklin Celebrini. The Sharks captain has enjoyed a whirlwind start to the season, from recording a hat trick in his first game after having the captain's C stitched onto his jersey (in preseason, but still), to leading San Jose through a remarkable 10-game winning streak that shows no signs of slowing down.

Yet as impressive as the on-ice accomplishments have been, they may not even qualify as the biggest headline of Celebrini's week: Less than 24 hours ago, the Sharks center publicly confirmed his romantic relationship with teammate, winger, and longtime best friend Will Smith—a revelation that quickly sent both the hockey world and social media into a frenzy.

Following today's game, we had the chance to talk to Celebrini for a few minutes.

Pond: Congratulations on today’s game! The team has now won ten in a row. What's been working so well during this stretch?

Celebrini: Thank you. Yeah, the team's been playing really well. I think everyone's just buying into what we're trying to do every night. We've been moving the puck well, making smart plays, and doing a good job supporting each other all over the ice. The forwards, the defensemen, the goalies, everyone shows up.

I think a big part of it is that we're playing as a group. Nobody's trying to do too much on their own. We've been sticking to our game plan, working hard, and trusting each other. There have definitely been games where things haven't been perfect, but we've found ways to stay composed and keep pushing.

At the end of the day, it's a long season, so you try not to get too caught up in the streak itself. We're happy with where we're at, but there's still a lot of hockey left to play. Right now, we're just focused on keeping that same mindset and taking it one game at a time.

Pond: You mentioned that even during a winning streak, not every game is going to be perfect. Tonight's first period seemed to be a good example of that. There were a few uncharacteristic mistakes, particularly from your line. What do you think was behind that slow start?

Celebrini: [laughs awkwardly, rubbing the back of his neck] Yeah, uh... sometimes that's just hockey, I guess. You're not going to come out flying every single night. There are games where things click right away, and there are games where it takes a little longer to find your legs.

[pauses for a moment, clearly considering his words] I mean, honestly, I think a lot of it was on me. I had a couple of turnovers early, missed a few plays I normally make, and that kind of stuff can snowball if you're not careful. The guys bailed me out a few times there.

It's just... [shrugs, cheeks noticeably reddening] it's been a lot lately. Not in a bad way, obviously. Just a lot of new things, a lot of attention, a lot to get used to. I'm still figuring out how to balance all of that while making sure I'm showing up the way I want to on the ice every night.

So yeah, I don't think there's some big secret behind it. I was a little off to start, and thankfully we have a really good team that helped turn things around.

In that, Celebrini is certainly correct—there are no more big secrets left to uncover.

For those who somehow missed it (and if you did… you may want to check your phone), news broke earlier today of Celebrini and Smith’s relationship announcement. The pair, who are now the first openly out active NHL couple, chose a refreshingly untraditional way of going public: less “official statement,” more “we’re not hiding this anymore.”

While many players tend to go the Instagram-post route for this kind of news, Celebrini and Smith opted for something far more straightforward. They simply stopped pretending otherwise.

Last night, the two were seen at one of San Jose’s more upscale restaurants (clearly making good use of those new matching NHL contracts). What initially looked like a quiet date night quickly became anything but. Within hours, images and clips from the evening began circulating widely online.

At first glance, an outside observer might have assumed it was an unfortunate leak of a private moment. But the footage tells a different story: the pair seated by the front window, unbothered by the visibility, sharing what can only be described as unapologetic heart-eyes over dessert, with Smith repeatedly kissing Celebrini’s hand mid-conversation.

Nothing about it reads accidental. If anything, it feels carefully unguarded: Less a slip into the public eye and more a deliberate step into it.

Pond: You know I have to ask, but I’ll keep it hockey-relevant. What’s it like playing hockey with your official boyfriend?

Celebrini: My b— I mean, it’s pretty good. It’s always good playing with Will. He’s a special player.

Pond: You’ve said that before.

Celebrini: Yeah… because it’s been true before.

Pond: And there’s no difference now that you’re officially dating?

Celebrini: Hockey is hockey?

Pond: Sure. But you’ve played with quite a few star players over the last year, McDavid and Crosby immediately come to mind. How would you say it compares playing with guys like that to playing with Smith?

Celebrini: Oh, I mean… Will is just really good at knowing where I am on the ice. And what I want to do. And it makes it easier for me to know what he wants to do too.

Obviously guys like Sid… I mean, he was my hero growing up, still is. Same with McDavid. Those are different conversations. But playing with Will is just… different, you know?

Pond: I don’t really. Can you explain that?

Celebrini: It’s like… you have to see it. Just by watching him you can tell he’s special. The way he reads the ice, the way he moves—everything is kind of… special [seems to catch himself mid-thought] It’s just easy to play with him. It always has been.

Pond: So you’re saying Will Smith is better at hockey than Sidney Crosby?

Celebrini: What? No—I mean, I didn’t say that. I don’t— [exhales, running a hand briefly across his mouth] I’m just saying it’s different. That’s all. Different chemistry. Different connection.

And as nice as it is to see the usually composed Sharks captain lose his footing for once, I don’t push it further. After all, he did just do something genuinely brave, and arguably quite risky for a community that hasn’t always embraced bravery with pride.

But my attempt to ease off the pressure doesn’t have the intended effect.

Pond: Don’t worry. It’s okay if you’re a little biased.

Celebrini: I’m not biased! I just know him really well. That’s all. I knew how special he was from the start. I saw him skate when I was like thirteen or fourteen, something like that. He was already ridiculous back then.

[stops, then adds, more firmly] And he is the best for me to play with. Like it’s been like this ever since we’ve been tog—I mean played together. Like on the same team. It’s not new.

It’s not often a room full of journalists collectively forgets how to do their job. But after Celebrini’s reveal, there’s a full, collective pause. Pens still. Phones steady. No follow-ups, no side remarks, no rustling papers. Just the weight of what was said hanging in a space that reporters are normally very quick to fill.

But it’s even less often that an NHL player, in the middle of a routine post-game interview, casually reframes what sounds an awful lot like the timeline of a long-running relationship with a teammate.

Maybe, given the way Celebrini and Smith have carried themselves over the years, it shouldn’t come as a surprise. Still, a surprise it is. Not necessarily at the fact itself, but at the ease with which it seems to have slipped into the open. Two players, still in their teens when this all supposedly began, now standing at the center of a story that has rewritten itself in real time.

For all the goals, streaks, and highlight-reel moments this season has already produced, it’s hard not to admit this might be the most unexpected development yet. And maybe the most impressive one.

There’s a brief reset in the room before the next question comes. I should clarify, for the sake of accuracy, that what follows was not quite what I intended to ask.

My question was meant to be straightforward: a hockey question, about Will Smith’s strengths on the ice, from someone who knows his game as closely as someone who has played with him for years. Celebrini, however, hears something slightly different.

Pond: What would you say is Smith’s best feature?

Celebrini: [quick with that one] His hands. Definitely his hands.

[nods once, then probably (and wrongly) assuming he’d have to elaborate] Like, you’d almost expect them to be smaller or something, maybe more delicate, I don’t know why… I think because he has, like, a tiny nose? So you kind of assume everything would be more, like… fine-detailed. But they’re not. They’re actually just really good hands. Like, really strong, really controlled. He’s got great touch with the puck. It’s honestly just… nice.

[a brief pause] Yeah. He’s got really nice hands.

The room reacts with a few quiet laughs, and Macklin Celebrini’s cheeks go a shade redder as he seems to register, a second too late, that the question may not have landed quite the way it was intended. Still, he’s already committed to the answer and there’s no sign of backing down.

That, in a way, is part of what makes this moment stand out.

Because even if the framing wasn’t exactly what I had in mind, I do think there’s something interesting in the fact that he understood it the way he did. For a long time, conversations around players’ personal lives have tended to sit in very familiar lanes: often discussed openly when they fit a certain expectation and handled more quietly when they don’t.

It’s not something unique to hockey, but hockey has certainly been no exception to it.

So, there’s something notable, and honestly refreshing, about a moment where that frame feels like it’s shifting. Where the question isn’t treated as unusual at all, but just… part of the game now.

And if Celebrini’s reaction is any indication, it doesn’t feel uncomfortable for him either. If anything, he seems pretty content in it. Which, given the season he’s having, probably shouldn’t come as a surprise. When things are going this well on the ice, there’s a sense that everything else tends to follow suit.

If Celebrini keeps up the current pace, Will Smith might want to start getting prepared for Celebrini to put a ring on his finger. Whether that is a cup ring or another or both? We leave that up to Celebrini. If there’s anything that’s become undeniable at this point, it’s that he knows exactly what he’s doing.
